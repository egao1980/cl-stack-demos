# score demo: the score model, ids, positions, validation, s-expressions, edits, views and the score builder

`harness-demos-score` is a tour of **score-protocol 0.3.0** from GHCR. Slice SC0 is the score model, slice SC1 adds the edit-operation algebra and views, and slice SC3 adds drafts: a score builder whose refs let a tie or a slur name a note that is not minted yet. The library models a notated score as an immutable value. A score holds metadata, a measure map shared by all parts, parts with staves and voices of events, a chord-symbol track and spanners. Every element has a stable id, and any element can carry provenance and a confidence. Every change is an edit operation, which is a value. Applying one returns a new score that shares everything untouched with the old one, plus a change set. Pitches, intervals, meters, keys, tempi and chord symbols are [music-theory](../music-theory/) 0.5 values (0.5.2 in the last run). Durations and offsets are rationals in whole notes: 1/4 is a quarter.

The demo builds one public-domain hymn tune in code: NEW BRITAIN, the tune of "Amazing Grace" (American folk melody, 1829; words by John Newton, 1779). It is in 3/4 and G major, with a one-beat pickup, and has two staves (melody and bass). Each section then uses that score. Section 11 also needs four voices, so it builds a short four-part fragment of OLD HUNDREDTH: the first line of Louis Bourgeois's melody (1551), with lower parts written for the demo. Section 12 builds the hymn's first line as a score of two parts, melody and bass. Everything is in [`src/demo.lisp`](src/demo.lisp). [`demo-output.txt`](demo-output.txt) is the output of the last run, on macOS arm64 with SBCL 2.6.9. Home and temp paths in it are replaced with `~` and `<tmp>`.

## Run

```bash
cd harness/score
cl-repo run -- sbcl --non-interactive --no-userinit --eval '(require "asdf")' \
  --eval '(asdf:load-system "harness-demos-score")' \
  --eval '(harness-demos-score:run-demos)' > demo-output.txt 2>&1
```

It needs SBCL and `cl-repo`, and nothing else. There is no network step once the packages are installed. A run takes about five seconds with `cl-repo run`, most of it start-up. The demo checks every printed result that has a known answer: 403 checks. `run-demos` returns `NIL` if any check fails or a section stops on an error, and CI then exits 1.

## What each section shows

1. **Building a score.** The tune is data: the melody and bass bar by bar, chord symbols at `(bar . offset)` positions, measure marks, and the two verses written hymnal style (`"A- maz- ing grace!"`). The constructors (`make-measure-info`, `make-note-event`, `make-lyric`, `make-chord-symbol-anchor`, …) turn the data into elements. The score has a pickup with a tempo mark (Moderato, ♩ = 84), and lyrics in two verses with `:begin` / `:middle` / `:end` syllables and extenders on the melismas. It has 18 chord symbols, among them N.C. and the slash chords G7/B and D7/F#. One chord sits under a tied note, where no note starts. The score also has a tie, five slurs, a breath mark, fermatas, dynamics, two rehearsal marks, and a repeat of bars 1-15 with first and second endings: verse 1 ends in the first ending, which also picks up verse 2. Ties and slurs name notes by id, so the score is built in two steps. `make-score` first mints ids for 169 elements. Then the tie (its start note is rebuilt with the same id) and the slurs are added through `copy-score`. The whole score is printed back as a table through the readers, and the elements are counted by class (175).
2. **Element ids.** Every id in document order: the measure map and its marks, the part, staves and voices, each event followed by its marks and lyrics, then the chord symbols and the spanners. The kind prefixes are `m`, `tm`, `rh`, `en`, `p`, `st`, `v`, `e`, `dy`, `ly`, `ti`, `br`, `fe`, `n`, `h` and `sp`, each followed by a base-36 serial. Step 1 numbered its elements 1 to 169 in document order. Step 2 continued the counter, so the tie got 170 (`ti-4q`), although it sits in bar 7. The section checks that the ids are unique, `valid-id-p` and XML NCNames, and that the same input mints the same ids. `copy-score` keeps every id and shares every element (`eq`). After the step-2 rebuild, 166 of the 169 elements are the same objects, and the three rebuilt ones (part, voice, note) keep their ids. A deleted chord symbol's id (`h-4k`) is not reused: the next one gets serial 176 (`h-4w`). It also covers `find-element` and `element-parent`, `unknown-element` with `use-value`, and slice ids (`m-c.v-3h`, a voice's share of a measure) with `find-slice`.
3. **Bars and positions.** The pickup is bar 0, so the score runs from bar 0 to bar 17 and lasts 13 whole notes. A table gives each bar's id, inherited time and key, length and start, and marks. `absolute-offset` and `offset-position` are shown on rational offsets (`(9 . 5/8)` is 55/8). The end of a bar is the start of the next, and `(1 . 0)` is the pickup's length. `element-position` places events, lyrics, a breath mark, the tie, a slur, measure marks and a chord symbol. Round trips cover all 59 events and all 313 offsets on the 1/24 grid. `invalid-position` is signalled for an offset past the pickup, a bar past the end, an absolute offset past 13, and the `bar-*` functions; a non-cons or float argument gives a `type-error`. `use-value` handlers clamp to the last position or to the score's duration. A score with no measures makes every position invalid.
4. **Validation.** `check-score` on a copy of the hymn with four mistakes, each made by rebuilding one element with its id kept: an overfull bar 3, a tie from D5 to a C5, a lyric on a bass rest, and a second ending that claims three measures on the last bar. It reports `:measure-duration` (detail `(:expected 3/4 :actual 1)`), `:tie-pitch`, `:misplaced-lyric` and `:bad-ending`, each with its subject id and bar, and it signals nothing while doing so. `validate-score` returns the valid hymn `eq`. On the broken copy it signals `score-invariant-violation` with the four issues, and `continue` resumes it with the score as it is.
5. **Provenance and confidence.** The same builder plays a recognition step that reads the tune from a page. Every melody note, lyric and chord symbol gets provenance `:omr` (backend, page), and seven elements also get a confidence and a bounding box. The recognised score has the same ids as the hymn but is not `score-equal` to it. `uncertain-elements` returns five elements below 0.8 in document order, two below 0.5 and seven below 1. After a reviewer confirms one note (rebuilt with confidence 1 and provenance `:user`), it drops out of the report. A confidence of 1.5 is a `type-error`.
6. **The canonical s-expression form.** The first 24 lines of `score-to-sexp` (14,494 characters in all), and `element-to-sexp` for a note with two lyrics and for a slash chord. The printed text is read back with `*read-eval*` NIL and `sexp-to-score`. The result is `score-equal` to the original, has the same counter, writes the same form, passes `check-score`, and mints the same next id. `score-equal` ignores the counter (999 is still equal) and counts everything else. Five malformed forms give `score-format-error` with format `:sexp` and the offending sub-form as position: an unknown key, the wrong kind under a key, an odd plist, a key given twice, and a top form that isn't `:score`. Errors from music-theory and from the constructors are not wrapped. `invalid-pitch` for `"H4"` is answered with `use-value` B4, a `type-error` for a negative duration with `store-value`, and the reading goes on.

7. **Editing.** Seven operations on the hymn, each applied to the result of the one before. Each step prints the constructor call, the operation's canonical form, the bars before and after, the change set (created, removed and modified ids, each with what it names), and how many elements are still the old objects (`eq`). The steps:
   - `set-pitch` turns bar 15's A4 into F#4. Only the note is modified. The note, its voice and its part are rebuilt, and the other 172 elements are shared.
   - `set-duration` makes bar 12's E4 a half note across the barline. The piece in bar 13 and the tie between the pieces are new. The D4 it covers is removed, and a rest fills the rest of that D4's time. The slur that ended on the D4 goes with the cascade. The new ids continue the counter: 176, 177, 178.
   - A shorter `set-duration` leaves a rest.
   - `insert-event` into the full first-ending bar signals `measure-overfull`, with voice, bar and excess, inside the restarts REFLOW, SKIP-EDIT and USE-VALUE. With REFLOW the voice re-flows: the D4 splits into tied eighths, and the final rest is cut at the end of the score.
   - `add-spanner` adds a slur, and `add-mark` a fermata.
   - Setting a lyric ("wretch" → "soul") is `add-mark` with a lyric. The new lyric takes the old lyric's id, and only the lyric is modified.

   Four refusals follow: `edit-target-missing`, a wrong kind (`invalid-edit`), `edit-position-invalid` and `edit-breaks-score`. Then SKIP-EDIT (the same score, `eq`) and USE-VALUE.
8. **Undo and composition.** `invert-edit` for each step: a table of the inverses' sizes, and step 1's inverse in full. Applied newest first, each inverse gives a score `score-equal` to the one before its step, with a mirrored change set (created ↔ removed, the same modified). The last one gives back the hymn. `compose-edits` makes the seven steps one operation, with the same result and one change set. Its inverse undoes all seven. A single operation composes to itself, nested composites are spliced, and the empty composite changes nothing. The `edit-to-sexp` form (637 characters) is read back with `*read-eval*` NIL and `sexp-to-edit`. It writes the same form, applies with an equal result and change set, and the inverses round-trip too. Three malformed forms give `score-format-error`.
9. **Re-barring.** `set-time` on bar 5 bounds the region. 3/4 → 6/8 on bars 1-4 keeps every barline (the same bar length): only bar 1's measure is modified, and the voice measures stay `eq`. 3/4 → 2/4 turns the four bars into six. Old bars 1 and 3 are kept, since a new bar starts where each of them started, and 2 and 4 are removed. Six new ties flow across the new barlines, and the chord symbols keep their absolute offsets (G7/B moves from `(2 . 1/2)` to `(3 . 1/4)`). The inverse restores the score. `set-time` 2/4 on bar 8 is refused, because it would drop bar 9 (rehearsal B) and bar 17 (the second ending). SKIP-EDIT returns the score unchanged.
10. **Transposition.** Up a major second: A major, with the chord symbols respelled (D7/F# → E7/G#). 77 elements are modified and the tie still holds. `:to-key` Ab major: Eb7/G, Ab chords. Up an augmented unison: G major gives G# major, eight sharps, which `parse-key` refuses. `choose-enharmonic-key` picks Ab major, so the result is `score-equal` to the `:to-key` one. The demo also shows `choose-enharmonic-key` on F#, C# and with `:max-accidentals 7`. A `:from`/`:to` range (bars 9-15) gives bar 9 its own A major. Bar 16 keeps G major, so the endings stay in G. Two refusals follow.
11. **Views.**
    - `extract-part` of the melody gives one staff, the same voice object, five slurs, 18 chord symbols, and an id map that sends every id to itself. The bass voice moves to staff 1 with the bass clef and keeps its events. The whole part gives the hymn again. An unknown id signals `unknown-element`, with USE-VALUE.
    - `condensed-view` turns the four-part OLD HUNDREDTH open score into two staves. S and A go on the treble staff, T and B on the bass staff, with stems up and down. The part and the staves are minted (`p-1c`, `st-1d`, `st-1e`) and every event is the source's. `extract-part` of the alto from the condensed view gives the alto alone, with stem NIL.
    - `unfold` works on an A B A form. One composite edit adds a Fine on the second ending, a four-bar interlude through `insert-measures`, D.C. al Fine and its chords. The walk gives 54 bars: 0-16 (pass 1), 1-15 (pass 2), 17-21, 0 (pass 2), 1-15 (pass 3), 17 (pass 2). It is printed as `(pass . bar)` and as runs. The view has no repeat signs or jumps left, and it is valid. A table maps the melody's onsets at the turns of the walk to view ids, source ids and passes. Unfolding the view again gives the same score. The plain hymn unfolds to 33 bars.
12. **Drafts: the score builder (0.3.0, SC3).** A tie or a slur names its notes by id, and `make-score` mints the ids, so section 1 needs two steps. A file reader has the same problem in every score: a tie is written on a note before the note it ends on. A `score-builder` hands out `draft-ref`s, which stand for ids not minted yet. An element *carries* a ref when the ref is its `:id`, and a tie's end or a spanner's start and end *refer* to one. The section covers:
    - **`builder-ref`.** It returns the same (`eq`) ref for keys that are `equal`, and another builder never returns that ref. `:prefer` sets a preferred id, and `nil` keeps it. `builder-id` before any build signals `unresolved-reference` ("not carried in a built score"), or returns `NIL` with `:errorp nil`.
    - **A two-part draft.** The first line of the hymn (the pickup and bars 1-8) becomes part "Melody" (verse 1) and part "Bass". It is built in one `make-score` call. The D5 of bar 7 is tied forward: its tie refers to the ref that the D5 of bar 8, built after it, carries. The two melisma slurs refer to refs carried by their notes. The parts' refs prefer `P1` and `P2`, as a reader would take them from a file. The score has 71 elements, no ref is left in it, and `check-score` finds nothing.
    - **The ids in document order.** `P1` and `P2` are the preferred ids. The other 69 elements are minted with serials 1 to 69, in document order. The tie `ti-17` sits in bar 7, before `e-19`, the note it ends on. In section 2 the tie got serial 170. A table gives each key, the id its carrier got (`builder-id`) and what refers to it.
    - **The builder's promise.** Build once with the preferred ids and the placeholder `"zz"` for every reference, read the minted ids off, and build again with them. The result is `score-equal` to the draft's score, with the same ids in the same order. The placeholder build reports `:spanner-endpoint` twice and `:tie-target` once.
    - **Preferred ids.** A given id wins: with the bass given `P1`, the melody part is minted `p-b`. With two carriers that prefer `P1`, the first in document order takes it. A preferred id that has a serial, `p-zz` (1295), raises the counter as a given id does, so minting starts at `m-100` (1296), even for the elements before the part.
    - **Errors.** A slur added through `copy-score` refers to two refs that nothing carries. `unresolved-reference` is signalled once per ref, in document order, and USE-VALUE supplies the ids. A ref carried by both parts is an error with no restart, and a build that signals records no ids. An edit whose elements hold a ref is refused as `invalid-edit` with "draft reference".
    - **An edit on the built score.** `add-mark` puts a fermata on the held D5. The change set holds one created and one modified id. 68 of the 71 elements are the old objects. Every subtree the edit does not touch is `eq`: the whole bass part, the measure map, the chord symbols, the slurs, the melody's staff, and its voice measures for bars 0-7. Only part `P1`, its voice, bar 8's voice measure and the note are rebuilt.
    - **The hymn in one call.** With refs for the tie and the five slurs, section 1's hymn is built by one `make-score` call. It has the same 175 elements in the same order, with serials 1 to 175 in document order. The tie is `ti-1y` (70), not `ti-4q` (170). The ids differ from the two-step build, so the scores are not `score-equal`.
    - **Two smaller changes.** `absolute-offset` now gives three reasons: "offset past the end of bar 0 (length 1/4)", "no such bar" and "negative offset". In 0.2.0 all three were "outside the score". `lossy-conversion` has a direction, `:to` (the default) or `:from`, and its report names it: "lossy conversion from musicxml: a trill dropped".

## Rough edges found

Filed on [egao1980/score-protocol](https://github.com/egao1980/score-protocol/issues):

- Fixed in 0.3.0: `absolute-offset` gave the reason "outside the score" for an offset past the end of its bar. `(0 . 1/2)` in a score with a quarter-note pickup names the instant 1/2, which is well inside the score. Section 3 now shows "offset past the end of bar 0 (length 1/4)". ([#2](https://github.com/egao1980/score-protocol/issues/2))
- Open: `invert-edit` rejects a direct restore as colliding with itself. This happens when the edit removed a spanner that is not the last, or changed a chord symbol in place. The inverse is then a restore of the whole score: 61 operations and 17 KB for step 2 of section 8, against one operation for the other steps. The result is still exact. ([#7](https://github.com/egao1980/score-protocol/issues/7))
- Known: after a jump, `unfold` plays only the last ending of each group, as its summary says, and as section 11 shows. Step 1 of the `views.lisp` header still reads otherwise. The PASS of a second ending's measure counts plays (1 the first time), not the pass it was selected for. ([#4](https://github.com/egao1980/score-protocol/issues/4))

The SC3 section (0.3.0) found nothing new. Everything else in SC0, SC1 and SC3 behaved as their file headers say.
