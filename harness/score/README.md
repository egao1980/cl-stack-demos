# score demo: the canonical score model, ids, positions, validation, s-expressions

`harness-demos-score` is a tour of **score-protocol 0.1.0** (slice SC0) from GHCR. The library models a notated score as an immutable value. A score holds metadata, a measure map shared by all parts, parts with staves and voices of events, a chord-symbol track and spanners. Every element has a stable id, and any element can carry provenance and a confidence. Pitches, meters, keys, tempi and chord symbols are [music-theory](../music-theory/) 0.4.0 values. Durations and offsets are rationals in whole notes: 1/4 is a quarter.

The demo builds one public-domain hymn tune in code: NEW BRITAIN, the tune of "Amazing Grace" (American folk melody, 1829; words by John Newton, 1779). It is in 3/4 and G major, with a one-beat pickup, and has two staves (melody and bass). Each section then uses that score. Everything is in [`src/demo.lisp`](src/demo.lisp). [`demo-output.txt`](demo-output.txt) is the output of the last run, on macOS arm64 with SBCL 2.6.9. Home and temp paths in it are replaced with `~` and `<tmp>`.

## Run

```bash
cd harness/score
cl-repo run -- sbcl --non-interactive --no-userinit --eval '(require "asdf")' \
  --eval '(asdf:load-system "harness-demos-score")' \
  --eval '(harness-demos-score:run-demos)' > demo-output.txt 2>&1
```

It needs SBCL and `cl-repo`, and nothing else. There is no network step once the packages are installed. A run takes about three seconds with `cl-repo run`, most of it start-up. The demo checks every printed result that has a known answer: 226 checks. `run-demos` returns `NIL` if any check fails or a section stops on an error, and CI then exits 1.

## What each section shows

1. **Building a score.** The tune is data: the melody and bass bar by bar, chord symbols at `(bar . offset)` positions, measure marks, and the two verses written hymnal style (`"A- maz- ing grace!"`). The constructors (`make-measure-info`, `make-note-event`, `make-lyric`, `make-chord-symbol-anchor`, …) turn the data into elements. The score has a pickup with a tempo mark (Moderato, ♩ = 84), and lyrics in two verses with `:begin` / `:middle` / `:end` syllables and extenders on the melismas. It has 18 chord symbols, among them N.C. and the slash chords G7/B and D7/F#. One chord sits under a tied note, where no note starts. The score also has a tie, five slurs, a breath mark, fermatas, dynamics, two rehearsal marks, and a repeat of bars 1-15 with first and second endings: verse 1 ends in the first ending, which also picks up verse 2. Ties and slurs name notes by id, so the score is built in two steps. `make-score` first mints ids for 169 elements. Then the tie (its start note is rebuilt with the same id) and the slurs are added through `copy-score`. The whole score is printed back as a table through the readers, and the elements are counted by class (175).
2. **Element ids.** Every id in document order: the measure map and its marks, the part, staves and voices, each event followed by its marks and lyrics, then the chord symbols and the spanners. The kind prefixes are `m`, `tm`, `rh`, `en`, `p`, `st`, `v`, `e`, `dy`, `ly`, `ti`, `br`, `fe`, `n`, `h` and `sp`, each followed by a base-36 serial. Step 1 numbered its elements 1 to 169 in document order. Step 2 continued the counter, so the tie got 170 (`ti-4q`), although it sits in bar 7. The section checks that the ids are unique, `valid-id-p` and XML NCNames, and that the same input mints the same ids. `copy-score` keeps every id and shares every element (`eq`). After the step-2 rebuild, 166 of the 169 elements are the same objects, and the three rebuilt ones (part, voice, note) keep their ids. A deleted chord symbol's id (`h-4k`) is not reused: the next one gets serial 176 (`h-4w`). It also covers `find-element` and `element-parent`, `unknown-element` with `use-value`, and slice ids (`m-c.v-3h`, a voice's share of a measure) with `find-slice`.
3. **Bars and positions.** The pickup is bar 0, so the score runs from bar 0 to bar 17 and lasts 13 whole notes. A table gives each bar's id, inherited time and key, length and start, and marks. `absolute-offset` and `offset-position` are shown on rational offsets (`(9 . 5/8)` is 55/8). The end of a bar is the start of the next, and `(1 . 0)` is the pickup's length. `element-position` places events, lyrics, a breath mark, the tie, a slur, measure marks and a chord symbol. Round trips cover all 59 events and all 313 offsets on the 1/24 grid. `invalid-position` is signalled for an offset past the pickup, a bar past the end, an absolute offset past 13, and the `bar-*` functions; a non-cons or float argument gives a `type-error`. `use-value` handlers clamp to the last position or to the score's duration. A score with no measures makes every position invalid.
4. **Validation.** `check-score` on a copy of the hymn with four mistakes, each made by rebuilding one element with its id kept: an overfull bar 3, a tie from D5 to a C5, a lyric on a bass rest, and a second ending that claims three measures on the last bar. It reports `:measure-duration` (detail `(:expected 3/4 :actual 1)`), `:tie-pitch`, `:misplaced-lyric` and `:bad-ending`, each with its subject id and bar, and it signals nothing while doing so. `validate-score` returns the valid hymn `eq`. On the broken copy it signals `score-invariant-violation` with the four issues, and `continue` resumes it with the score as it is.
5. **Provenance and confidence.** The same builder plays a recognition step that reads the tune from a page. Every melody note, lyric and chord symbol gets provenance `:omr` (backend, page), and seven elements also get a confidence and a bounding box. The recognised score has the same ids as the hymn but is not `score-equal` to it. `uncertain-elements` returns five elements below 0.8 in document order, two below 0.5 and seven below 1. After a reviewer confirms one note (rebuilt with confidence 1 and provenance `:user`), it drops out of the report. A confidence of 1.5 is a `type-error`.
6. **The canonical s-expression form.** The first 24 lines of `score-to-sexp` (14,494 characters in all), and `element-to-sexp` for a note with two lyrics and for a slash chord. The printed text is read back with `*read-eval*` NIL and `sexp-to-score`. The result is `score-equal` to the original, has the same counter, writes the same form, passes `check-score`, and mints the same next id. `score-equal` ignores the counter (999 is still equal) and counts everything else. Five malformed forms give `score-format-error` with format `:sexp` and the offending sub-form as position: an unknown key, the wrong kind under a key, an odd plist, a key given twice, and a top form that isn't `:score`. Errors from music-theory and from the constructors are not wrapped. `invalid-pitch` for `"H4"` is answered with `use-value` B4, a `type-error` for a negative duration with `store-value`, and the reading goes on.

## Rough edges found

Filed on [egao1980/score-protocol](https://github.com/egao1980/score-protocol/issues):

- Open: `absolute-offset` gives the reason "outside the score" for an offset past the end of its bar. `(0 . 1/2)` in a score with a quarter-note pickup names the instant 1/2, which is well inside the score. Section 3 shows it. ([#2](https://github.com/egao1980/score-protocol/issues/2))

Everything else in SC0 behaved as its file headers say.
