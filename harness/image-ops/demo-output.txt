== 1. The 0.2.0 operations, and the test card
==============================================================================
image-protocol 0.2.0's editing operations (crop-image, rotate-image, flip-image,

== 2. What reaches BACKEND-RESIZE (R3, R4)
==============================================================================
A backend with a native resampler specialises BACKEND-RESIZE. REC below records each
call and declines (answers NIL), so the pure-Lisp resampler still does the work. The
caller's image itself arrives (EQ, not a copy), and the three keys are always passed
(R4): the filter (:lanczos3 when none is given), the reducing gap the pure-Lisp
resampler would apply, and LINEAR as T or NIL. card is a 96 × 72 :rgb test card,
card-rgba the same in :rgba.

  (resize-image card 24 18 :backend rec)
    => an image, 24 × 18 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 24 18 :filter :lanczos3 :reducing-gap nil :linear nil)
  (resize-image card 24 18 :filter :bicubic :reducing-gap 2 :linear :yes :backend rec)
    => an image, 24 × 18 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 24 18 :filter :bicubic :reducing-gap 2 :linear t)
  (resize-image card 24 18 :reducing-gap 1.7 :backend rec)
    => an image, 24 × 18 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 24 18 :filter :lanczos3 :reducing-gap 1.7 :linear nil)
  The gap goes to the backend as the caller gave it (EQL: 2 stays 2, 1.7 stays a
  single float). An image with alpha gets no gap, as in Pillow, and neither does
  :nearest:
  (resize-image card-rgba 24 18 :filter :bicubic :reducing-gap 2 :backend rec)
    => an image, 24 × 18 :rgba
    called (backend-resize rec card-rgba 24 18 :filter :bicubic :reducing-gap nil :linear nil)
  (resize-image card 24 18 :filter :nearest :reducing-gap 3 :backend rec)
    => an image, 24 × 18 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 24 18 :filter :nearest :reducing-gap nil :linear nil)
  thumbnail and fit-image pass :backend on to resize-image, with their own default
  gap of 2. A backend listed twice is consulted twice:
  (thumbnail card 32 32 :backend rec)
    => an image, 32 × 24 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 32 24 :filter :lanczos3 :reducing-gap 2 :linear nil)
  (fit-image card :max-width 192 :enlarge t :backend rec)
    => an image, 192 × 144 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 192 144 :filter :lanczos3 :reducing-gap 2 :linear nil)
  (resize-image card 24 18 :filter :box :backend (list rec rec))
    => an image, 24 × 18 :rgb
    called (backend-resize rec card 24 18 :filter :box :reducing-gap nil :linear nil)
    called (backend-resize rec card 24 18 :filter :box :reducing-gap nil :linear nil)

  Nothing to do, no call (R3): the input itself comes back.
  (resize-image card 96 72 :backend rec)
    => an image, 96 × 72 :rgb
    no BACKEND-RESIZE call
  (thumbnail card 200 200 :backend rec)
    => an image, 96 × 72 :rgb
    no BACKEND-RESIZE call
  (fit-image card :max-width 96 :backend rec)
    => an image, 96 × 72 :rgb
    no BACKEND-RESIZE call

When a backend answers, its pixels are the result (R5, R6). The result is a fresh
COPY-IMAGE of the caller's image with the answer's pixel vector, so the caller's
metadata stays and the answer object itself is not returned. marker answers with
every sample 128; photo is card with orientation 6 and gamma 0.45455, as a decoder
would return it.

  (resize-image photo 24 18 :backend marker) => an image, 24 × 18 :rgb
  (every (lambda (v) (= v 128)) (image-pixels result)) => t
  (list (image-orientation result) (image-gamma result)) => (6 0.45455)
  (list (image-orientation answer) (image-gamma answer)) => (nil nil)
  (eq result answer) => nil
  (eq (image-pixels result) (image-pixels answer)) => t
  (equalp (image-pixels photo) before) => t

== 3. Declining backends: the pure-Lisp result, byte for byte (R5)
==============================================================================
A backend declines by answering NIL, and the method on IMAGE-BACKEND always does.
plain has no BACKEND-RESIZE method of its own; rec is the recorder of section 2.
With :backend (list plain rec) both are consulted, in order, both decline, and the
pure-Lisp resampler runs: the result is 0.2.0's, the same samples as :backend nil.
Each cell: a 64 × 48 test card resized to 23 × 17 with :reducing-gap 2.

  filter     :gray       :gray-alpha :rgb        :rgba
  :nearest   same        same        same        same
  :box       same        same        same        same
  :bilinear  same        same        same        same
  :bicubic   same        same        same        same
  :lanczos3  same        same        same        same

  same => 20
  (length (calls rec)) => 20

== 4. Bad answers and errors: IMAGE-CORRUPT and TRY-NEXT-BACKEND (R5, R7)
==============================================================================
Any answer other than NIL must be a fresh image of the requested size, in the
input's format, depth 8, with the right number of octets and a pixel vector that is
not the input's own (R5). Otherwise RESIZE-IMAGE signals IMAGE-CORRUPT, with the
backend and a cause naming the broken rule. card is the 96 × 72 :rgb test card.

  a vector, not an image, resize to 24 × 18
    => IMAGE-CORRUPT
    cause: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: (SIMPLE-ARRAY (UNSIGNED-BYTE 8) (1296)), not NIL or an IMAGE
    message: Could not decode nil with #<recording-backend a vector, not an image>: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: (SIMPLE-ARRAY (UNSIGNED-BYTE 8) (1296)), not NIL or an IMAGE
  10 × 10 instead of 24 × 18, resize to 24 × 18
    => IMAGE-CORRUPT
    cause: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: width 10, expected 24
    message: Could not decode nil with #<recording-backend 10 × 10 instead of 24 × 18>: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: width 10, expected 24
  :gray for an :rgb input, resize to 24 × 18
    => IMAGE-CORRUPT
    cause: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: format :GRAY, expected :RGB
    message: Could not decode nil with #<recording-backend :gray for an :rgb input>: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: format :GRAY, expected :RGB
  depth 16, resize to 24 × 18
    => IMAGE-CORRUPT
    cause: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: depth 16, expected 8
    message: Could not decode nil with #<recording-backend depth 16>: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: depth 16, expected 8
  the input's own pixels as 72 × 96 (the same length as 96 × 72), resize to 72 × 96
    => IMAGE-CORRUPT
    cause: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: IMAGE's own pixel vector, not a fresh one
    message: Could not decode nil with #<recording-backend the input's own pixels as 72 × 96 (the same length as 96 × 72)>: BACKEND-RESIZE answer: IMAGE's own pixel vector, not a fresh one

The message reads "Could not decode nil with ...": IMAGE-CORRUPT keeps the report
of IMAGE-DECODE-ERROR, and the answer check gives no :format. That is open as
image-protocol#8; the condition type, the backend and the cause are right.

RESIZE-IMAGE establishes the restart TRY-NEXT-BACKEND around each backend's call and
the check of its answer (R7). It treats that backend as having declined, so the next
one runs, or the pure-Lisp resampler after the last. Below, gray answers :gray for
:rgb, and a handler-bind takes the restart:

  restart report: Treat #<recording-backend gray> as declining: consult the next resize backend, or the pure-Lisp resampler.
  :backend (list gray stb), restart taken => the same pixels as :backend stb: T
  :backend gray, restart taken            => the same pixels as :backend nil: T

A condition the backend signals itself reaches the caller unchanged: the protocol
handles nothing (R7). failing signals IMAGE-NATIVE-UNAVAILABLE, as stb does when
stbir_resize fails (S7); the same restart recovers from it.

  unhandled => IMAGE-NATIVE-UNAVAILABLE: Native image library unavailable: the native call failed
  with try-next-backend => the same pixels as :backend stb: T

== 5. *RESIZE-BACKENDS* against :BACKEND (R1, R2, R8)
==============================================================================
A backend resizes only when the caller names it, through :backend or by binding
*resize-backends*, the default of :backend in resize-image, thumbnail and fit-image.
Registering a backend never changes the samples resize-image returns. Loading
image-backend-stb registered an stb backend for decoding and encoding:

  *resize-backends* => nil
  (mapcar #'type-of (image-backends)) => (stb:stb-backend)
  (same-pixels-p default (resize-image card 24 18 :filter :bicubic :backend nil)) => t
  (same-pixels-p default (resize-image card 24 18 :filter :bicubic :backend stb)) => nil

Binding *resize-backends* opts in; an explicit :backend wins over the binding, and
nil there means the pure-Lisp resampler (counts are calls to rec and other):

  (let ((*resize-backends* rec)) ...)
    (resize-image card 24 18)                                      rec 1, other 0
    (resize-image card 24 18 :backend nil)                         rec 0, other 0
    (resize-image card 24 18 :backend other)                       rec 0, other 1
    (fit-image card :max-width 48)                                 rec 1, other 0
    (thumbnail card 32 32 :backend (list other rec))               rec 1, other 1
  (eq *resize-backends* rec) => t
  (let ((*resize-backends* (list rec other))) ...)
    (thumbnail card 32 32)                                         rec 1, other 1
    (thumbnail card 200 200)                                       rec 0, other 0
  The operations change no special variable and register nothing (R8):
  *resize-backends* => nil
  (length (image-backends)) => 1

A designator is NIL, one IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of them (R1). Anything else
is IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT with argument BACKEND and the designator itself as the
value. It is checked after the 0.2.0 arguments and before anything else, so it is
reported even when nothing would be resized (R2). listed is (list rec 42), dotted
is (cons rec rec).

  (resize-image card 24 18 :backend :stb)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: resize: bad backend :STB: NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  (resize-image card 24 18 :backend 'stb-backend)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: resize: bad backend IMAGE-BACKEND-STB:STB-BACKEND: NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  (resize-image card 24 18 :backend listed)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: resize: bad backend (#<recording-backend rec> 42): NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  (resize-image card 24 18 :backend dotted)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: resize: bad backend (#<recording-backend rec> . #<recording-backend rec>): NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  (resize-image card 96 72 :backend :protocol)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: resize: bad backend :PROTOCOL: NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  (thumbnail card 200 200 :backend 7)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: thumbnail: bad backend 7: NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  (let ((*resize-backends* :fast)) (fit-image card :max-width 48))
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: fit: bad backend :FAST: NIL, an IMAGE-BACKEND or a proper list of IMAGE-BACKENDs
  The 0.2.0 checks come first: a bad width is reported before a bad designator.
  (resize-image card 0 18 :backend :stb)
    => IMAGE-INVALID-ARGUMENT: resize: bad width 0: a positive integer

== 6. stb: :bilinear and :bicubic served, the rest declined (S1, S2)
==============================================================================
image-backend-stb 0.3.0 adds a BACKEND-RESIZE method on stb_image_resize2. It serves
:bilinear (stb's triangle filter) and :bicubic (Catmull-Rom: Keys' cubic with
a = -0.5, Pillow's BICUBIC) without :linear, in every pixel format, and declines the
rest (S2): :lanczos3 (stb has no Lanczos-3 kernel), :box (stb's box is a trapezoid),
:nearest (no gain) and :linear t (no Pillow reference). A declined call falls back to
the pure-Lisp resampler, byte for byte. Below, a counting stb backend resizes a
320 × 240 :rgb test card to 80 × 60; the difference is against :backend nil, in
sample values.

  filter     linear  stb         same as pure Lisp      max / mean difference
  :nearest   nil     declined    same                     0.00 0.000
  :box       nil     declined    same                     0.00 0.000
  :bilinear  nil     answered    close, not equal         1.00 0.051
  :bicubic   nil     answered    close, not equal         3.00 0.276
  :lanczos3  nil     declined    same                     0.00 0.000
  :bilinear  t       declined    same                     0.00 0.000
  :bicubic   t       declined    same                     0.00 0.000

  :bicubic in every format, 64 × 48 → 23 × 17 (S2, S6):
  :gray        stb answered, result 23 × 17 :gray depth 8, input unchanged: T
  :gray-alpha  stb answered, result 23 × 17 :gray-alpha depth 8, input unchanged: T
  :rgb         stb answered, result 23 × 17 :rgb depth 8, input unchanged: T
  :rgba        stb answered, result 23 × 17 :rgba depth 8, input unchanged: T

== 7. Closeness: pure Lisp, stb and Pillow 11.3.0 (S8)
==============================================================================
stb resamples in floats with no rounding between its passes and clamps the kernel
at the edges where Pillow renormalises it, so its samples are close to Pillow's, not
equal (S8). Each row compares three results of the same test card: the pure-Lisp
resampler (:backend nil), stb (:backend stb) and Pillow 11.3.0, run through
`uv run --with pillow==11.3.0 python -I crosscheck/pillow_resize.py`. Differences are
in sample values, the colour of alpha formats premultiplied, as S8 measures them.
S8's bounds: max 43, mean 3.2. gap is :reducing-gap, which stb accepts and does
not apply.

                                             Lisp – Pillow  stb – Lisp     stb – Pillow
  case                                       max   mean     max   mean     max   mean
  :rgb 1024×768 → 256×192 :bicubic             0.00 0.000     3.00 0.050     3.00 0.050
  :rgb 1024×768 → 256×192 :bilinear            0.00 0.000     1.00 0.025     1.00 0.025
  :rgb 1024×768 → 333×250 :bicubic             0.00 0.000     9.00 0.015     9.00 0.015
  :rgb 512×384 → 128×96 :bicubic gap 2         0.00 0.000     5.00 0.780     5.00 0.780
  :gray 200×150 → 640×480 :bilinear            0.00 0.000     1.00 0.053     1.00 0.053
  :gray-alpha 160×120 → 53×40 :bicubic         0.00 0.000    10.74 0.249    10.74 0.249
  :rgba 320×240 → 80×60 :bicubic               0.00 0.000     3.97 0.242     3.97 0.242
  :rgba 64×48 → 160×120 :bilinear              0.00 0.000     1.89 0.154     1.89 0.154

  Pillow 11.3.0. Lisp – Pillow is 0 in every row: the pure-Lisp resampler gives Pillow's samples.
