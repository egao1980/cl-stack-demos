# music-theory demo: pitches and intervals, meter and rhythm, keys and Roman numerals, chord symbols, spelling and key detection

`harness-demos-music-theory` takes a short tour of **music-theory 0.5.0** from GHCR. The library treats tonal music theory as values and exact arithmetic. Sections 1-6 cover slice MT0 (spelled pitches, the line of fifths and intervals), sections 7-11 slice MT1 (meters, the metric hierarchy, dots and tuplets, tie splitting, tempo), sections 12-16 slice MT2 (keys and scales, scale degrees, Roman numerals, mixture, a progression), sections 17-22 slice MT3 (chord symbols: a lead-sheet chart, dialects, Unicode and slash chords, essential tones, transposition, errors), and sections 23-25 slice MT4, new in 0.5.0 (spelling MIDI numbers, key detection, transposition to a key and enharmonic keys). Durations and positions are rationals in whole notes: 1/4 is a quarter, 3/8 a dotted quarter. Everything is in [`src/demo.lisp`](src/demo.lisp). [`demo-output.txt`](demo-output.txt) is the output of the last run, on macOS arm64 with SBCL 2.6.9. Home and temp paths in it are replaced with `~` and `<tmp>`.

## Run

```bash
cd harness/music-theory
cl-repo run -- sbcl --non-interactive --no-userinit --eval '(require "asdf")' \
  --eval '(asdf:load-system "harness-demos-music-theory")' \
  --eval '(harness-demos-music-theory:run-demos)' > demo-output.txt 2>&1
```

It needs SBCL and `cl-repo`, and nothing else: no network once the package is installed, and no model. It runs in about a second. Sections 23 and 24 also read the MT4 test corpus that ships with the package (`tests/fixtures/`) and recount its figures. The demo checks each printed result that has a known answer (530 checks: 123 for MT0, 104 for MT1, 171 for MT2, 52 for MT3, 80 for MT4). `run-demos` returns `NIL` if any check fails or a section stops on an error, and CI then exits 1.

## What each section shows

1. **Spelled pitches.** `parse-pitch` and `pitch-name` in ASCII (`F#4`, `Bbb3`, `C-1`) and Unicode (`F♯4`, `B𝄫3`). The parser takes `x` for a double sharp, either letter case and surrounding blanks. A `-` is never a flat: in `B-1` it belongs to octave -1. The section also shows a round trip over all 35 spellings in octaves -1 to 9 (385 names), readable `#S(...)` printing, and `pitch-class-name`.
2. **MIDI and the scientific octave.** The octave belongs to the letter: B#3 and C4 are both MIDI 60, but they are not `equalp`. `pitch-enharmonic-p` says they sound the same, and `interval-between` C4 and B#3 is `-d2`, not a unison. The section also shows the staff position (`pitch-diatonic-index`) and the pitch class.
3. **The line of fifths.** A table of positions -15 to 19 (Fbb to B##), and the twelve pitch classes with their two or three spellings, twelve positions apart. Each interval is a fixed distance on the line (P5 +1, M2 +2, A4 +6, d5 -6). Over all 1225 pairs of spellings, the distance and the simple interval name determine each other.
4. **Intervals.** `interval-between` with its name, quality, number, direction, compound flag and simple form (E♭4 to A4 is A4, C4 to G5 is P12, A4 down to C3 is -M13). `parse-interval` and `interval-name` round-trip 161 names and reject the 22 impossible ones (P3, M4, …). The section shows `simple-interval`, `invert-interval` (numbers add up to 9) and `reverse-interval`. A unison is read as ascending, so reversing A1 gives d1.
5. **Transposition keeps the spelling.** F#4 up M2 is G#4, and up d3 it is Ab4. The C major scale goes up an A4 to F# major (with E#) and up a d5 to Gb major (with Cb): the same MIDI numbers, but no shared spelling. Twelve fifths up from C1 end on B#7, not C8. Moving p by `(interval-between p q)` gives q for all 11,025 pairs of 105 pitches.
6. **Conditions and restarts.** `invalid-pitch` carries the text and a reason. `handler-bind` answers `use-value nil` for bad input, and the interactive `use-value` reads replacement text from `*query-io*`. Since 0.3.0 `"Cbbb4"`, `"C♯♭4"` and `"Cx#4"` give the reason "too many or mixed accidentals" (they used to say "missing octave"), and so do `parse-pitch-class` and `parse-key`. `parse-interval "M4"` is given `P4` through `use-value`. Transposing G# major up A1 fails on F##5, which would need three sharps (`invalid-pitch`, no restart). An interval with no name still transposes. One `music-theory-error` handler catches every case.
7. **Meters.** `parse-meter` reads `4/4`, `6/8`, `12/8`, the additive `2+3/8` and the symbols `C` and `C|` (printed `4/4` and `2/2` by `meter-string`). `meter-beats` gives two dotted-quarter beats for 6/8, three eighths for 3/8 (simple) and `(1/4 3/8)` for 2+3/8, whose `beat-unit` is `NIL`. `compound-p` holds for 6/8, 9/8, 12/8 and 6/4. A table covers twelve meters. `invalid-meter` gives a reason for each bad text and takes a `use-value`. A leading zero (`04/4`) is accepted, which [#3](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/3) leaves open.
8. **The metric hierarchy.** `metric-weight` maps of one bar on the 1/16 grid for 4/4, 6/8 and 2+3/8, with a dot column per position (the downbeat 0, the half bar of 4/4 at 1, the beats at 2, eighths 3, sixteenths 4), then 3/4, 12/8, 2/2 and 3+5/8 as rows of weights. Since 0.3.0 a beat of n units splits into its n units, so the five-eighth group of 3+5/8 has an eighth grid (weight 3) instead of eighths off the grid (9). The same offset 1/4 weighs 2 in 4/4, 3 in 6/8 and 1 in 2+3/8. Offsets are taken modulo the bar (pickups, later bars), and a triplet position weighs 9.
9. **Dots and tuplets.** `duration-dots` gives the base and dots (7/16 is a double-dotted quarter; 5/8, 1/12 and 1/20 are no single value), `tuplet-duration` the sounding length under n/m (a triplet eighth is 1/12, a quintuplet 16th 1/20, a duplet eighth in 6/8 3/16), and `duration-dots :tuplet` turns it back (180 of 180 bases, dots and ratios).
10. **Tie splitting.** `split-duration` on a syncopated two-bar 4/4 rhythm and a two-bar 6/8 rhythm, drawn on the 1/16 grid as given and as tied (4/4 1/2 at 1/8 → 1/8 + 1/4 + 1/8; 6/8 1/2 at 0 → 3/8 + 1/8; a quarter across beat 2 of 6/8 → two eighths). Then `max-dots`, barlines, a pickup, 12/8 and 2+3/8, and a sweep over every 1/16 onset and every duration up to 2 in nine meters, 3+5/8 among them (4416 notes): the pieces add up, each is one value with at most one dot, none crosses a barline, and nothing inside a piece is stronger than its start. A tuplet length signals `music-theory-error`. Two drawn bars of 3+5/8 end the section: an eighth inside the five-eighth group is one note (0.2.0 rejected it), and a quarter across the start of the group is tied there.
11. **Tempo and phrase windows.** `make-tempo`, `duration-seconds` and `seconds-duration` at ♩ = 72 (a quarter is 5/6 s, three 4/4 bars 10 s) and for 6/8 with a dotted-quarter beat, exact round trips over 320 durations and tempi, and `max-window-duration`: with a breath at least every 8 seconds at ♩ = 96, a phrase on an eighth-note grid can last 25/8 (three bars and one eighth, 7.8125 s). A table gives the same window at ♩ = 60 to 144.
12. **Keys, key signatures and scales.** `parse-key` reads `"Eb major"` and `"F# minor"` and the short forms (`"G"` major, `"g"` and `"Gm"` minor; a mode word in any case), and `key-name` prints the long form in ASCII or Unicode. A table gives the 15 signatures from Cb major (7 flats) to C# major (7 sharps) with their relative minors, and lists each scale's altered notes, which are the run of the line of fifths from F# up or Bb down. All 30 keys parse back from their names. `invalid-key` gives a reason (`"G#"` and `"Fb"` need eight accidentals, `"Gmaj"`, `"G maj"`) and takes a `use-value`. `make-key` builds any key (G# major has F## as its 7th) but `key-signature` refuses it. `scale-degrees` shows natural, harmonic and melodic A, C and F# minor side by side; in all 15 minor keys the harmonic scale raises the 7th of the natural one and the melodic scale lowers the 3rd of the parallel major.
13. **Scale degrees.** `degree-of` returns the degree, the alteration against the scale and the octave of the tonic below: in D major F#4 is degree 3 and Gb4 degree 4 lowered; G#4 is #7 in natural A minor and 7 in harmonic A minor. The chromatic scale in C major (sharps up, flats down) and in A minor for each variant, written as altered degrees (#4, b6, b7). `pitch-of-degree` goes back (degree 2 of B major in octave 4 is C#5), and the round trip holds in all 15,750 cases: 30 keys, three variants, 35 spellings as classes and in octaves 2-5.
14. **Roman numerals.** `parse-roman` and `roman-name` for I, ii6, V7, vii°7, V/V, vii°7/V, bVI and N6, with the slots of each value, and `chord-for` in C major and in A minor (V/V is B D# F# in A minor; bVI there is Fb major, because VI is already F). In a minor key the case of the numeral picks the raised or natural 6th and 7th (vi is F# A C#, VI is F A C). Other spellings print canonically (`V6/5` → `V65`, `V2` → `V42`, `iiø7` → `ii/o7`), and `invalid-roman-numeral` gives a reason for `V9`, `V/V/V`, `V7/V7`, `Ger65` and others. `roman-for` reads chords literally (F# A C Eb is `#ivo7`, D7/F# is `II65`) and with `:applied-to` (`viio7/V`, `V65/V`, `V7/vi`, `V42/IV`). Sweeps: all 109,340 well-formed numerals, with and without targets, parse back from both names; `roman-for` of `chord-for` gives every numeral back in all 30 keys (46,200 cases) and for every applied numeral in C major and A minor (215,600), except where `chord-for` cannot spell a tone with two accidentals.
15. **Mixture.** `roman-mixture-p` is true for bVI, iv and bVII in C major and false for IV, N6 (Db is not in C minor either) and V/V; iv/V is mixture, judged in G major. A table spells bVI, iv and bVII in all 15 major keys, each equal to VI, iv or VII of the parallel minor. In C minor, I, ii, IV, vi and viiø7 are mixture; V and vii°, from the harmonic minor, are not.
16. **A progression, to chords and back.** I–vi–ii6–V7–I in G major becomes G B D, E G B, A C E over C, D F# A C, G B D; `roman-for` reads every chord back to its numeral, and the bass line G E C D G is degrees 1 6 4 5 1. The same numerals give the progression in Eb and F# major, and i–VI–iio6–V7–i in E minor.
17. **A lead-sheet chord chart parsed.** `| C  Am7 | Dm7  G7 | Cmaj7  A7b9 | D9sus4  G13 | C6/9  N.C. |` is split at bar lines and spaces, and `parse-chord-symbol` reads each symbol into a `chord-symbol`: root, quality, extensions (G13 is a dominant seventh with `(9 11 13)`), alterations (`A7b9` has `((9 . -1))`), suspension and added tones (C6/9 is a sixth chord with an added 9). N.C. is the no-chord value. `chord-tones` gives each chord's tones, spelled from the root (A7b9 is A C# E G Bb; D9sus4 has G instead of F#), and `print-chord-symbol` the canonical text (`A7(b9)`).
18. **Dialects.** `Cdim`, `C°`, `C-7`, `CΔ7`, `Cø`, `C7alt` and `C+` read under `:classical`, `:pop` and `:jazz`. Only one reading differs: `dim`, `o` and `°` alone are the diminished triad in `:classical` and the diminished seventh in the other two. Each value is printed back in all three dialects: the diminished triad is `C°`, `Cm(b5)` and `C-(b5)`, the major seventh `CM7`, `Cmaj7` and `CΔ7`, and the altered dominant `C7alt` only in `:jazz`. A sweep reads 15,147 texts (3 roots × 51 quality words × 33 endings × 3 dialects) and prints the 2,388 distinct values in each dialect and style. All 14,328 round trips give the same value and text back. Up to 0.4.0, 90 of them failed ([#10](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/10)): a diminished triad or half-diminished seventh without its 5 prints in `:pop` as `Cm(b5,omit5)`, which rule R5 rejected. Since 0.5.0 R5 accepts a written b5 with omit5 when the flat fifth names the quality, in either order, so `Cm(b5,omit5)` reads back as `C°(omit5)` and `Cm7(b5,omit5)` as `Cø7(omit5)`. `C7(b5,omit5)` and `Cm(#5,omit5)` still break R5.
19. **Unicode and ASCII, slash chords.** `F♯m7♭5` and `F#m7b5` are the same value, a half-diminished seventh (m7 with b5 folds into ø7). It prints as `F#m7(b5)` or `F♯m7(♭5)`, and as `F#ø7` in `:jazz`, where the words stay fixed in both styles. Six ASCII and Unicode pairs, double sharps and flats among them, read as one value. In a modifier `-` is a flat and `+` a sharp (`C7-9`, `C7+5`). For slash chords the bass can be a chord tone (`G7/B` has the 3 in the bass, `Dm7/C` the 7) or not (`C/F#`, `Ab/Bb`, `D/C`). `chord-tones` lists only the chord's own tones, and `C6/9/E` is the 6/9 chord over E.
20. **Essential tones.** `chord-essential-tones` with n = 3 and n = 4 for G13, C7#9, Fm11 and Bø7: what a three-voice choir or a guitar trio would keep. G13 keeps B F E (3, 7, 13), C7#9 E Bb D#, Fm11 Ab Eb Bb, and Bø7 D A F, the b5 before the root. A table gives the full priority order for 15 chords and the tones it never lists: the 11 of a dominant 13th and the 3 of an eleventh chord with a major third. A suspension takes the 3's place, and the altered and added tones go where the quality's row puts them.
21. **Transposing chord symbols.** `Ebmaj7 Cm7 Fm7 Bb7/D Gm7 C7b9 Fm7/Ab Bb13 Eb6/9` is moved up a M2, from Eb major to E major (`B7/D#`, `F#m7/A`), and up a m2 for comparison (`Cb7/Eb`, `Gbm7/Bbb`: the same sounds, spelled from Fb). Root and bass move on the line of fifths and the other slots stay as they are. `TEXT` becomes `NIL`, the tones follow the root (C#7(b9) is C# E# G# B D), and N.C. stays N.C. The progression is shown in all 15 major keys: each chord comes back when transposed back to Eb, and every tone moves by the same distance on the line of fifths as the tonic (135 chords).
22. **Chord-symbol errors and restarts.** `chord-symbol-parse-error` for `Cmaj8` (position 4, after `maj`: "a modifier, "(", "/" or the end of the symbol") and `Hm7` (position 0: "a root letter A-G or N.C."). Eleven more are each drawn with a caret under the position, among them one for each rule R1-R6 (`Cm7sus4`, `C+(b5)`, `C7(b9,b9)`, `C9(add9)`, `C7(b5,omit5)`, `C7b9alt`). The error offers the restarts `use-value` and `skip-chord-symbol`. A chart with four bad symbols is read with a handler that logs each error and skips the symbol. A second handler reads the German H as B through `use-value` (`Hm7` → `Bm7`) and skips the rest. `Cdom7(b9)` signals the `unknown-chord-quality` warning (TEXT `"dom7"`): with `use-value :dominant-7` the result is `C7(b9)`, and muffled it is a major triad, `C(b9)`. One `music-theory-error` handler also catches an unprintable value and `invalid-pitch` for a tone or transposed root beyond two accidentals.
23. **Spelling a MIDI melody.** `spell-midi` and `spell-sequence` turn MIDI numbers into spelled pitches by distance on the line of fifths, with rules S0-S7 (previous note, key, one side, consistency, semitone resolution, no augmented or diminished step, center, tie). One note in C major with no context gives C C# D Eb E F F# G G# A Bb B, each with its ambiguity d/6 (D 0, G# 1). A table shows each rule on a short phrase (D D# E and E Eb D in C major, E# kept in B minor). The opening of The Entertainer (Joplin, 1902) as 36 MIDI numbers is spelled with no key hint (C major is detected) and with `:key` C major, the same notes and per-note ambiguities either way; the D#5 has ambiguity 5/6, but S4 spells it D# because it steps up to E. Moved up a semitone, down a semitone and by a tritone, the tune comes out in Db, B and F# major without a hint, and in C#, Cb and Gb major with one (D##5 E#5 in C# major): the C major text moved by `transpose-to-key`, every note keeping its ambiguity. `ambiguous-spelling` (MIDI 68 alone in C major, Ab4 or G#4) is signalled with `signal`, so it is silent unhandled; the section shows its slots, the restarts `use-value` (Ab4; a spelling of another MIDI number is a `type-error`) and `muffle-warning` (G#4), and a logging handler that runs once in `(68 60 68 67)`, the second 68 following the first by S3. The package's 50-melody test corpus is recounted: 1699 of 1699 notes with and without the key, 3398 of 3398 in 100 transposed copies with the key (3072 without). Last, the open [#14](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/14): Purcell's lament bass G F# F E Eb D comes out with Fb in G minor.
24. **Key detection.** `pitch-class-histogram` of `(pitch . duration)` events, bare pitches, spelled pitches and pitch-class names, exact, by duration or by `:count` (Twinkle, Twinkle: 2 0 2 0 2 2 0 3 0 1 0 0 whole notes). `detect-key` returns all 24 keys with their Pearson r, best first. Jingle Bells under the three profiles: Krumhansl-Kessler puts A minor first (0.673), Temperley and Aarden F major. A table of seven public-domain tunes (Twinkle, Für Elise, Greensleeves, Jingle Bells, Drunken Sailor, The Entertainer, Mozart's Symphony No. 40) gives the first key under each profile: 4, 6 and 6 of 7 match the notated key, and the misses are the relative minor, the parallel mode, the subdominant and Greensleeves' G naturals. Für Elise is E minor by count and A minor by duration. Over the 50-melody corpus the top-1 and top-2 counts are those the tests hold: Krumhansl-Kessler 39 and 47, Temperley 45 and 49, Aarden 41 and 48. A caller's own profile (scale membership) ties C major and F major for Twinkle, and the tie keeps candidate order. The chromatic scale gives `NIL`, a constant profile `music-theory-error`, and bad arguments `type-error`.
25. **Transposing to a key; enharmonic keys.** `transpose-to-key` keeps scale degree and alteration (F#4 from C to D major is G#4, to Db major G4, to C# major F##4), moves the tonic's letter at most three steps (C4 to G is G3, to F# F#4, to Gb Gb3) and ignores the modes (E4 from C major to A minor is C#4). Eight bars of Greensleeves with chord symbols go from A minor to E minor, Eb minor and D# minor: the melody through `transpose-to-key`, the chords through `transpose-chord-symbol` with the same two keys, so every melody note on a chord tone stays that chord tone, spelled the same (84 of 84; the raised 7th is D# under B7, D under Bb7, C## under A#7), roots agree, and each moves back exactly. `choose-enharmonic-key` for the ten keys with an enharmonic twin, with the default, `:prefer :sharps`, `:prefer :flats` and `:max-accidentals 7`: D♭ against C♯ (C# major becomes Db major), G♭ against F♯ (both kept as given, or chosen by `:prefer`), Cb major to B major, A# minor to Bb minor; G# major built with `make-key` becomes Ab major. The Entertainer up an A1, in the key `choose-enharmonic-key` picks, equals what `spell-sequence` gave for the raised MIDI numbers.

## Rough edges found

Filed on [egao1980/music-theory](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues):

- Fixed in 0.3.0: `parse-pitch "Cbbb4"` (also `"C♯♭4"` and `"Cx#4"`) gave the reason "missing octave", although the octave is there and the extra accidental is what is wrong. Section 6 now shows the new reason. ([#4](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/4))
- Fixed in 0.3.0: in an additive meter, a beat group of five (or 7, 9, …) eighths was halved at 5/16, so its eighths weighed 9, off every grid, and `split-duration` rejected a plain eighth in `3+5/8` with "not notatable without a tuplet". Sections 8 and 10 now show the eighth grid and the eighth. ([#7](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/7))
- Open: a leading zero in a meter (`04/4`) is accepted, among other MT1 follow-ups. Section 7 shows it. ([#3](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/3))
- Fixed in 0.5.0: a diminished triad or half-diminished seventh without its 5 printed in `:pop` (the triad in `:jazz` too) as `Cm(b5,omit5)` or `Cm7(b5,omit5)`, and rule R5 rejected that text when it was read back, breaking 90 round trips of the sweep. Section 18 now shows 0 failures and the texts read back. ([#10](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/10))
- Open: a chromatic step can be spelled far from the key, because S4 makes a semitone step diatonic and S5 avoids the augmented unison: the lament bass G F# F E Eb D gets Fb in G minor, and D C# C gets B# in D major (also Cb and B# in D minor, C## and Db in E minor, Cb in F major). The 50-melody corpus has no such line. The end of section 23 shows it. ([#14](https://github.com/egao1980/music-theory/issues/14))

The MT2 and MT3 sections found no new issues; the MT4 sections found #14.
