========================================================================
== 1. Reading a hymn
========================================================================
READ-MUSICXML takes a pathname, a string of MusicXML, UTF-8 octets or a
character stream, and returns a score-protocol SCORE: an immutable value with
a measure map, parts with staves and voices of events, a chord-symbol track
and the spanners. Durations are rationals in whole notes (1/4 is a quarter).

The input is NEW BRITAIN, the tune of "Amazing Grace" (American folk melody,
1829; words by John Newton, 1779), re-encoded by hand for this demo: the first
phrase pair, sung twice. It has a one-beat pickup, two verses of lyrics,
<harmony> chord symbols, a tie, two slurs, a dynamic, a tempo mark and a repeat
with first and second endings. Some elements carry id attributes. The file,
scores/new-britain.musicxml:

    => t   ; a character stream

== 2. Hymnal voices: SPLIT-VOICES
========================================================================
A hymnal prints soprano and alto on the treble staff and tenor and bass on the
bass staff. Where two voices move together they share a stem: one chord carries
both. Where their rhythms part, the staff switches to two voices (MusicXML
<voice> 1 and 2, stems up and down). The excerpt is the first line of OLD
HUNDREDTH (melody by Louis Bourgeois, 1551; words "All people that on earth do
dwell" by William Kethe, 1561), with alto, tenor and bass written for the demo.
Bars 0 and 1 are shared-stem chords. The last note of bar 1 is a unison: soprano
and alto both sing D4, printed as one note. In bar 2 the alto holds half notes
under the soprano's quarters, as voice 2. From scores/old-hundredth-satb.musicxml,
bar 1 of the treble staff and bar 2:

   32        <note><chord/><pitch><step>D</step><octave>4</octave></pitch><duration>1</duration><voice>1</voice><type>quarter</type><stem>up</stem><staff>1</staff></note>
   33        <note><pitch><step>F</step><alter>1</alter><octave>4</octave></pitch><duration>1</duration><voice>1</voice><type>quarter</type><stem>up</stem><staff>1</staff><lyric><syllabic>end</syllabic><text>ple</text></lyric></note>

Read as written, the part has the file's three voices: 1 and 2 on the treble
staff, 5 on the bass staff, holding chords:

  (setf *as-written* (read-musicxml (score-file "old-hundredth-satb.musicxml")))

    v-6   staff 1 :up      | G4+D4:h | G4+D4:q F#4+D4:q E4+B3:q D4:q | G4:q A4:q B4:h |
    v-v   staff 1 :down    |  |  | D4:h D4:h |
    v-y   staff 2 :down    | B3+G2:h | B3+G2:q A3+D3:q G3+E3:q G3+B2:q | G3+B2:q F#3+D3:q G3+G2:h |

With :SPLIT-VOICES :AUTO, READ-MUSICXML calls SPLIT-VOICES on the score it read.
Per staff, the upper voice keeps the top note of each shared-stem chord and a
lower voice gets the bottom one. The bass staff had one voice, so its lower voice
is new. A single note in a shared-stem bar is a unison: both voices sing it, and
the lower voice's copy is a guess, signalled as LOW-CONFIDENCE-GUESS:

  (read-musicxml (score-file "old-hundredth-satb.musicxml") :split-voices :auto)

  S v-6   staff 1 :up      | G4:h | G4:q F#4:q E4:q D4:q | G4:q A4:q B4:h |
  A v-v   staff 1 :down    | D4:h | D4:q D4:q B3:q D4:q | D4:h D4:h |
  T v-y   staff 2 :up      | B3:h | B3:q A3:q G3:q G3:q | G3:q F#3:q G3:h |
  B v-1o  staff 2 :down    | G2:h | G2:q D3:q E3:q B2:q | B2:q D3:q G2:h |

  signalled: low-confidence-guess: low-confidence guess for "e-1n" (unison): 1/2

  (element-position *satb* (low-confidence-guess-element-id (first guesses)))
    => (1 . 3/4)   ; bar 1, beat 4: the alto's D4
  (element-confidence
   (find-element *satb* (low-confidence-guess-element-id (first guesses))))
    => 1/2
  (provenance-backend
   (element-provenance
    (find-element *satb* (low-confidence-guess-element-id (first guesses)))))
    => "score-backend-musicxml"
  (mapcar #'element-id (uncertain-elements *satb*))
    => ("e-1n")   ; the one uncertain element

Ids follow the notes. The soprano keeps the chord events' ids, the alto takes
the ids of the lower chord notes, and the new bass voice is minted:

  as written, bar 0 treble: chord e-7, notes n-8=G4 n-9=D4
  split, bar 0: soprano e-7, alto n-9; voice ids v-6 v-v v-y v-1o

  (score-equal (split-voices *as-written*) *satb*)
    => t   ; the same as calling SPLIT-VOICES yourself
  (check-score *satb*) => nil   ; no issues
  (score-equal (split-voices *satb*) *satb*)
    => t   ; a split score has nothing left to split

========================================================================
== 3. Chord symbols: <harmony> and music-theory
========================================================================
A <harmony> is a root, a <kind>, optional <degree>s and an optional bass.
HARMONY-TO-CHORD-SYMBOL turns the element into a music-theory CHORD-SYMBOL, and
CHORD-SYMBOL-TO-HARMONY turns a symbol back into an element. The chord-level
mapping is music-theory 0.5.1's (CHORD-SYMBOL-MUSICXML-KIND and
MUSICXML-KIND-CHORD-SYMBOL); this backend only adapts it to XML. Each row reads
a <harmony>, prints the symbol, writes it back, and reads that again:

  <kind> (root, degrees, bass)               reads as          written as
  major (C)                                  C                 major
  minor (A)                                  Am                minor
  diminished (F#)                            F#m(b5)           diminished
  augmented (Eb)                             Eb+               augmented
  dominant (G)                               G7                dominant
  major-seventh (F)                          Fmaj7             major-seventh
  minor-seventh (D)                          Dm7               minor-seventh
  half-diminished (B)                        Bm7(b5)           half-diminished
  diminished-seventh (C#)                    C#dim7            diminished-seventh
  major-minor (A)                            Ammaj7            major-minor
  major-sixth (Bb)                           Bb6               major-sixth
  dominant-ninth (A)                         A9                dominant-ninth
  suspended-fourth (D)                       Dsus4             suspended-fourth
  power (E)                                  E5                power
  Neapolitan (Db)                            Db                major
  dominant (G; /B)                           G7/B              dominant
  dominant (D; add 9 -1)                     D7(b9)            dominant (add 9 -1)
  major (A; subtract 3 0, add 4 0; /E)       Asus4/E           suspended-fourth
  major (A; add 4 0, subtract 3 0; /E)       A(add4,omit3)/E   major (add 4 0, subtract 3 0)
  none (C)                                   N.C.              none

Neapolitan, and the other kinds named after their use, are spellings of chords
the model already has: Neapolitan on Db is a Db major triad, written back as
major. Degrees are ordered: major with subtract 3, add 4 is a suspension, Asus4/E,
which music-theory writes with its own kind, suspended-fourth. In the other
order, add 4, subtract 3, it is an added fourth and an omitted third. The
D7(b9) row as XML:

    <harmony>
      <root>
        <root-step>D</root-step>
      </root>
      <kind>dominant</kind>
      <degree>
        <degree-value>9</degree-value>
        <degree-alter>-1</degree-alter>
        <degree-type>add</degree-type>
      </degree>
    </harmony>

  (multiple-value-list (chord-symbol-musicxml-kind (parse-chord-symbol "D7(b9)")))
    => ("dominant" ((:add 9 -1)))   ; music-theory's side of the mapping
  (print-chord-symbol
   (musicxml-kind-chord-symbol "major" (parse-pitch-class "A") :bass
                               (parse-pitch-class "E") :degrees
                               '((:subtract 3 0) (:add 4 0))))
    => "Asus4/E"

use-symbols. MusicXML's <kind> has display attributes: use-symbols="yes" asks for
symbols (Δ for major seventh, - for minor, ° and ø), and text gives the exact
text to print. Both say how the chord was printed, not which chord it is, so
the reader ignores them and the writer never emits them. Three spellings of a
C major seventh:

    <harmony><root><root-step>C</root-step></root><kind>major-seventh</kind></harmony>
      => Cmaj7
    <harmony><root><root-step>C</root-step></root><kind use-symbols="yes">major-seventh</kind></harmony>
      => Cmaj7
    <harmony><root><root-step>C</root-step></root><kind text="&#916;7">major-seventh</kind></harmony>
      => Cmaj7

  (print-chord-symbol
   (harmony-to-chord-symbol
    (harmony-element
     (harmony-xml "C" 0 "major-seventh" :kind-attributes " use-symbols=\"yes\"")))
   :dialect :jazz :accidentals :unicode)
    => "CΔ7"   ; symbols are the printer's choice: music-theory's :jazz dialect

A symbol parsed from text remembers that text and its dialect. MusicXML has no
place for them, so CHORD-SYMBOL-TO-HARMONY returns what it lost as a second value
(WRITE-MUSICXML turns that into a LOSSY-CONVERSION warning):

  (nth-value 1 (chord-symbol-to-harmony (parse-chord-symbol "C-7" :dialect :jazz)))
    => ("chord-symbol text")
  (print-chord-symbol
   (harmony-to-chord-symbol
    (chord-symbol-to-harmony (parse-chord-symbol "C-7" :dialect :jazz))))
    => "Cm7"   ; the same chord, printed in the default dialect

A harmony the model can't hold is MUSICXML-UNSUPPORTED (section 4), here from a
direct call, so with no path. <kind>other</kind> spells a chord by its degrees
alone:

  (harmony-to-chord-symbol
   (harmony-element
    "<harmony><root><root-step>C</root-step></root><kind>other</kind></harmony>"))
    -> musicxml-unsupported: musicxml format error: a chord spelled by its degrees alone (kind other)
  (handler-bind ((musicxml-unsupported #'skip-element))
    (harmony-to-chord-symbol
     (harmony-element
      "<harmony><root><root-step>C</root-step></root><kind>other</kind></harmony>")))
    => nil   ; SKIP-ELEMENT drops it

========================================================================
== 4. Content outside the model: MUSICXML-UNSUPPORTED
========================================================================
The reader maps every MusicXML element it knows to the model, drops layout,
playback and bookkeeping elements without a word, and treats everything else as
content the model can't hold. Here is the hymn again with one such element, a
trill on "grace!" in bar 2:

   47        <note><pitch><step>B</step><octave>4</octave></pitch><duration>4</duration><voice>1</voice><type>half</type>
   48          <notations><ornaments><trill-mark/></ornaments></notations>
   49          <lyric number="1"><text>grace!</text></lyric>

By default (:UNSUPPORTED :ERROR) the read stops with MUSICXML-UNSUPPORTED, a
SCORE-FORMAT-ERROR naming the element and its path:

  (setf *trill* (trill-text))
  (read-musicxml *trill*)
    -> musicxml-unsupported: musicxml format error: outside the MusicXML mapping at "/score-partwise[1]/part[1]/measure[3]/note[1]/notations[1]/ornaments[1]"

  (musicxml-unsupported-element c) => "ornaments"
  (score-format-error-position c)
    => "/score-partwise[1]/part[1]/measure[3]/note[1]/notations[1]/ornaments[1]"
  (score-format-error-format c) => :musicxml
  (score-error-reason c) => "outside the MusicXML mapping"

The error is signalled inside a SKIP-ELEMENT restart. A handler that invokes it
drops the element, and reading goes on as if it weren't in the file:

  (block nil
    (handler-bind ((musicxml-unsupported
                    (lambda (c)
                      (return
                       (mapcar #'restart-name (subseq (compute-restarts c) 0 1))))))
      (read-musicxml *trill*)))
    => (skip-element)   ; the innermost restart
  (setf *skipped*
          (handler-bind ((musicxml-unsupported #'skip-element))
            (read-musicxml *trill*)))

  (score-equal *skipped* *hymn*) => t   ; the hymn without the trill, ids and all
  (string= (write-musicxml *skipped*) (write-musicxml *hymn*))
    => t   ; byte for byte when written
  (search "ornament" (write-musicxml *skipped*)) => nil   ; no trace of it

The :UNSUPPORTED keyword sets the policy for the whole read. :WARN drops each
such element with a LOSSY-CONVERSION warning naming it. Its report says "to
musicxml" although nothing is being written (egao1980/score-protocol#9). :SKIP
drops the element silently:

  (read-musicxml *trill* :unsupported :warn)
    warns: lossy-conversion: lossy conversion to musicxml: <ornaments> dropped
    => a score, SCORE-EQUAL to *hymn*: t

  (read-musicxml *trill* :unsupported :skip)
    conditions signalled: 0
    => a score, SCORE-EQUAL to *hymn*: t

  (read-musicxml *trill* :unsupported :ignore)
    -> simple-type-error: The value of score-backend-musicxml::unsupported is :ignore, which is not of type (member :error :warn :skip).

Through serdes-protocol the error arrives as SERDES-DECODE-ERROR. Since
serdes-protocol 0.3.0 it is signalled in place: its SERDES-ERROR-CAUSE is the
reader's condition, and a handler outside DECODE can still take SKIP-ELEMENT
(the serdes demo shows this at length):

  (serdes-protocol:decode *trill* :format :musicxml)
    -> serdes-protocol:serdes-decode-error: Serdes error: decode failed for :MUSICXML: musicxml format error: outside the MusicXML mapping at "/score-partwise[1]/part[1]/measure[3]/note[1]/notations[1]/ornaments[1]"
  (score-equal
   (handler-bind ((serdes-protocol:serdes-decode-error #'skip-element))
     (serdes-protocol:decode *trill* :format :musicxml))
   *hymn*)
    => t   ; SKIP-ELEMENT from a handler around DECODE

== 5. Writing: .musicxml, .mxl and serdes formats
========================================================================
WRITE-MUSICXML returns the MusicXML text (or writes it to a pathname or a
stream). It writes the DOCTYPE, which no reader needs to fetch, a <player> per
voice, and every element id as an id attribute on its carrier. The first lines
for the hymn:

  (setf *written* (write-musicxml *hymn*))

    1  <?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
    2  <!DOCTYPE score-partwise PUBLIC "-//Recordare//DTD MusicXML 4.0 Partwise//EN" "http://www.musicxml.org/dtds/partwise.dtd">
    3  <score-partwise version="4.0">
    4    <work>
    5      <work-title>Amazing Grace</work-title>
    6    </work>
    7    <movement-title>NEW BRITAIN</movement-title>
    8    <identification>
    9      <creator type="composer">American folk melody, 1829</creator>
   10      <creator type="lyricist">John Newton, 1779</creator>
   11      <rights>Public domain. This encoding: MIT.</rights>
   12    </identification>
   13    <part-list>
   14      <score-part id="melody">
   15        <part-name>Melody</part-name>
   16        <player id="v-b">
   17          <player-name/>
   18        </player>
   19      </score-part>
   20    </part-list>
   21    <part id="melody">
   22      <measure number="0" implicit="yes" id="pickup.melody">
        ...

  id attributes in the written file, by element:
    score-part 1, player 1, part 1, measure 10, clef 1, direction 2, harmony 10, note 20, lyric 28, slur 2, tied 1, barline 2, fermata 1

  (length (text-lines *written*)) => 482   ; lines; the input has 119

Each of the 79 elements has its id on its carrier (the part's id is on <score-part>
and, as the reference, on <part>), so a read gives the same score back, ids
included. The written text is also a fixed point: written twice it is the same
bytes, and reading and writing it again changes nothing.

  (score-equal (read-musicxml *written*) *hymn*) => t   ; read, write, read
  (string= (write-musicxml *hymn*) (write-musicxml *hymn*)) => t   ; byte-stable
  (string= (write-musicxml (read-musicxml *written*)) *written*)
    => t   ; a fixed point
  (set-exclusive-or (carried-ids *written*)
                    (mapcar #'element-id (score-elements *hymn*)) :test #'string=)
    => nil   ; every element id is in the file, once

  (pathname-name (write-musicxml *hymn* (tmp-path "new-britain.musicxml")))
    => "new-britain"   ; with a pathname it writes the file
  (score-equal (read-musicxml (tmp-path "new-britain.musicxml")) *hymn*) => t

A score whose elements have provenance or confidence loses them in MusicXML.
Writing warns once per kind of loss. The split SATB score of section 2 has one
guess:

  (write-musicxml *satb*)
    warns: lossy-conversion: lossy conversion to musicxml: provenance and confidence dropped

  (length
   (uncertain-elements
    (read-musicxml
     (handler-bind ((warning #'muffle-warning))
       (write-musicxml *satb*)))))
    => 0   ; the voices are kept, the confidence isn't

.mxl. WRITE-MXL stores the same text in a zip archive: an uncompressed mimetype
entry first, as MusicXML 4.0 asks, then META-INF/container.xml naming the
rootfile, then the score. Every entry is stored with no timestamp, so the bytes
depend on the score only.

  (setf *mxl* (write-mxl *hymn*))

  (length *mxl*) => 13115   ; octets
  (equalp (write-mxl *hymn*) *mxl*) => t   ; byte-stable

  entry                    method  size
  mimetype                      0  34
  META-INF/container.xml        0  188
  score.musicxml                0  12555

  mimetype: application/vnd.recordare.musicxml
  META-INF/container.xml:
    <?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
    <container>
      <rootfiles>
        <rootfile full-path="score.musicxml" media-type="application/vnd.recordare.musicxml+xml"/>
      </rootfiles>
    </container>

  (score-equal (read-mxl *mxl*) *hymn*)
    => t   ; READ-MXL finds the rootfile through container.xml
  (pathname-type (write-mxl *hymn* (tmp-path "new-britain.mxl"))) => "mxl"
  (score-equal (read-mxl (tmp-path "new-britain.mxl")) *hymn*) => t
  (read-mxl (babel:string-to-octets *written* :encoding :utf-8))
    -> score-format-error: mxl format error: not a zip archive

serdes-protocol. Loading the system registers two formats, :MUSICXML (text) and
:MXL (binary). ENCODE and DECODE delegate to the functions above:

  (serdes-protocol:format-media-type :musicxml)
    => "application/vnd.recordare.musicxml+xml"
  (serdes-protocol:format-media-type :mxl) => "application/vnd.recordare.musicxml"
  (serdes-protocol:find-format-for-media-type "application/vnd.recordare.musicxml")
    => :mxl
  (serdes-protocol:format-binary-p :mxl) => t
  (string= (serdes-protocol:encode *hymn* :format :musicxml) *written*) => t
  (score-equal (serdes-protocol:decode *written* :format :musicxml) *hymn*) => t
  (equalp (serdes-protocol:encode *hymn* :format :mxl) *mxl*) => t
  (score-equal (serdes-protocol:decode *mxl* :format :mxl) *hymn*) => t

== 7. External entities are refused
========================================================================
XML external entities (XXE) let a document pull in a local file or a URL. The
reader parses with xml-backend-native, which never fetches anything: it refuses
an external entity at its declaration, and the reader reports that as a
SCORE-FORMAT-ERROR whose CAUSE is the XML-ENTITY-ERROR. The secret here is a
file in the run's temp directory:

    1  <?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
    2  <!DOCTYPE score-partwise [
    3    <!ENTITY secret SYSTEM "file://<tmp>/secret.txt">
    4  ]>
  ...

  (read-musicxml *xxe*)
    -> score-format-error: musicxml format error: external entity declaration at (3 . 24)

  (condition-line (score-format-error-cause c))
    => "xml-protocol:xml-entity-error: XML error: external entity declaration at 3:24"
  (score-format-error-position c) => (3 . 24)   ; line 3, column 24: the declaration
  (search "42" (princ-to-string c)) => nil   ; the secret is nowhere in the report

The refusal comes at the declaration, before any reference is read: a
document that declares the entity and never uses it is refused just the same.
A parameter entity naming a URL is refused at its declaration too. Had either
been fetched, the error would be about a file or a connection (port 9 has no
listener):

    <!DOCTYPE score-partwise [<!ENTITY secret SYSTEM "file:///etc/hostname">]>
  (read-musicxml *unused*)
    -> score-format-error: musicxml format error: external entity declaration at (2 . 48)

    <!DOCTYPE score-partwise [<!ENTITY % remote SYSTEM "http://127.0.0.1:9/evil.dtd"> %remote;]>
  (read-musicxml *parameter*)
    -> score-format-error: musicxml format error: external entity declaration at (2 . 50)

Internal entities are not expanded either: xml-backend-native knows only the
predefined entities and numeric character references. So the "billion laughs"
document, eight nested entities that would expand to 20 million characters,
stops at its first reference:

    <!DOCTYPE score-partwise [<!ENTITY a "ha"><!ENTITY b "&a;&a;&a;&a;&a;&...
  (read-musicxml *laughs*)
    -> score-format-error: musicxml format error: unknown entity &h; at (3 . 49)

The DOCTYPE every MusicXML file starts with names an external DTD subset by URL.
The parser refuses to fetch it with an XML-ENTITY-ERROR that offers CONTINUE,
and READ-MUSICXML takes that restart, so the files of sections 1 and 2 read
without a network. Through xml-protocol directly:

  (handler-bind ((xml-protocol:xml-entity-error #'continue))
    (xml-protocol:decode *hymn-text*))
    signalled: xml-protocol:xml-entity-error: XML error: external DTD subset is not fetched at 2:121, CONTINUE taken

== 8. The 0.1.1 fixes: spanner stops, unison bars
========================================================================
score-backend-musicxml 0.1.1 fixes two reader rules. Each case below gives the
input, what 0.1.0 read (from the 0.1.1 contract and its issue), and what this
release reads.

(a) Spanner stops close their own voice first (score-backend-musicxml#3).
MusicXML numbers slurs per part, and asks for distinct numbers while two
overlap, but some programs number them per staff or per voice. Then two open
slurs share a number. scores/minuet-in-g.musicxml has the first two bars of the
Minuet in G major, BWV Anh. 114 (Christian Petzold, 1725; left hand simplified),
with a slur in each hand, both number 1. The right hand's opens in bar 1
(voice 1), the left hand's opens after the <backup> (voice 5). Each closes in bar
2, the right hand first:

   23        <note><pitch><step>G</step><octave>4</octave></pitch><duration>1</duration><voice>1</voice><type>eighth</type><stem>up</stem><staff>1</staff><notations><slur type="start" number="1"/></notations></note>
   28        <note><pitch><step>G</step><octave>3</octave></pitch><duration>4</duration><voice>5</voice><type>half</type><stem>down</stem><staff>2</staff><notations><slur type="start" number="1"/></notations></note>
    ...
   32        <note><pitch><step>D</step><octave>5</octave></pitch><duration>2</duration><voice>1</voice><type>quarter</type><stem>down</stem><staff>1</staff><notations><slur type="stop" number="1"/></notations></note>
   36        <note><pitch><step>B</step><octave>3</octave></pitch><duration>6</duration><voice>5</voice><type>half</type><dot/><stem>down</stem><staff>2</staff><notations><slur type="stop" number="1"/></notations></note>

  (read-musicxml (score-file "minuet-in-g.musicxml"))

    v-5   staff 1 nil      | D5:q G4:e A4:e B4:e C5:e | D5:q G4:q G4:q |
    v-e   staff 2 :down    | G3:h A3:q | B3:h. |

  slur G4:e (e-7, voice v-5) -> D5:q (e-b, voice v-5)
  slur G3:h (e-f, voice v-e) -> B3:h. (e-h, voice v-e)

  (length spanners) => 2
  (mapcar (lambda (sp) (same-voice-p score sp)) spanners)
    => (t t)   ; each slur within one hand
  (mapcar
   (lambda (sp)
     (list (event-text (find-element score (spanner-start sp)))
           (event-text (find-element score (spanner-end sp)))))
   spanners)
    => (("G4:e" "D5:q") ("G3:h" "B3:h."))
  (check-score score) => nil

0.1.0 paired each stop with the slur of its number opened last, whatever its
voice. The right hand's stop (D5, bar 2) closed the left hand's slur, and the
left hand's stop (B3) closed the right hand's:
  0.1.0  slur  G3:h (left hand) -> D5:q (right hand)
         slur  G4:e (right hand) -> B3:h. (left hand)
The same cross-wiring put the soprano's crescendo in OLD HUNDREDTH's corpus
file onto the bass. Wedges follow the same rule as slurs. A stop with no open
spanner of its number in its own voice still closes the one opened last, as
before. Written back, the slurs keep their ends:

  (score-equal again score) => t   ; read, write, read

(b) Unison bars are split too (score-backend-musicxml#4). In hymnal layout, a
bar in which soprano and alto sing the same notes is printed as single notes:
one notehead, no chord, and no second voice. The SATB excerpt of section 2 is
changed so: WITHOUT-ALTO-IN-BAR-2 drops the alto's own notes in bar 2 (voice 2
and its <backup>), so the treble staff of bar 2 holds only G4 A4 B4. Bars 0 and

  (count-if (lambda (line) (search "<voice>2</voice>" line)) (text-lines text)) => 0

  (read-musicxml (without-alto-in-bar-2 ...) :split-voices :auto)

  A v-1k  staff 1 :down    | D4:h | D4:q D4:q B3:q D4:q | G4:q A4:q B4:h |
  T v-v   staff 2 :up      | B3:h | B3:q A3:q G3:q G3:q | G3:q F#3:q G3:h |
  B v-1p  staff 2 :down    | G2:h | G2:q D3:q E3:q B2:q | B2:q D3:q G2:h |

  (voice-pitches split 1)
    => (("D4:h") ("D4:q" "D4:q" "B3:q" "D4:q") ("G4:q" "A4:q" "B4:h"))   ; the alto: bar 2 in unison with the soprano
  (voice-pitches split 0)

   (lambda (g)

   guesses)
    => (((1 . 3/4) "unison" 1/2) ((2 . 0) "unison" 1/2) ((2 . 1/4) "unison" 1/2) ((2 . 1/2) "unison" 1/2))   ; each unison copy is a guess
  (length (uncertain-elements split)) => 4
  (check-score split) => nil

    => t   ; the same through SPLIT-VOICES
